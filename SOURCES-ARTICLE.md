**How to read the status labels**
- **Read and quoted:** the agent opened the document during a run and quoted it, and code confirmed the quote appears word for word in the text it read (see `checks.py`).
- **Snippet only:** only a search-result excerpt was seen. Treat as a lead, and open the page before relying on it.
- **Secondary:** a news story, law-firm alert or summary about a primary document. Law firms write for clients in the industry, so check the primary source.
- **My reading:** an inference, not a quote.

## 1. Kalshi is the party deciding who won, and its decision is final within Kalshi

- **Kalshi Rulebook, Rule 6.3(c), p. 58** (read and quoted): "Kalshi has sole discretion to interpret a Contract's Terms and Conditions" and "Determinations of the Outcome Review Committee are final and not subject to review."
- **Kalshi Rulebook, Rule 7.1(c), p. 59** (read and quoted): "The Outcome Review Committee has full discretion in resolving the Market Outcome Review Process. The determinations made by the Outcome Review Committee are final."
- Document: Kalshi Rulebook, CFTC filing copy: https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules07012525155.pdf (the filing's title reads v1.17; earlier project notes call the copy v1.18).
- Not established: who sits on the Outcome Review Committee. The text read does not say.

## 2. An affiliate of Kalshi and market makers that earn fee rebates also trade on Kalshi

- **Member Agreement §VII.C** (read and quoted): Kalshi's board approved "an affiliate of Kalshi, to be a member of Kalshi". Document: https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-member-agreement.pdf
- **Rulebook Rule 2.12(e), p. 22** (read and quoted): the affiliate's "business purpose is profitability, and liquidity provision is a significant part of its business model". Also that its "capitalization derives from the holding company that owns the Company and Entity".
- **Member Agreement §VI.T** (read and quoted): market makers can receive fee discounts and rebates, including a "revenue share from fees".
- **Rulebook Rule 4.5(a), p. 41** (read and quoted): on contracts where market makers must quote prices, they "will have Position Accountability Levels that are 10 times the Position Accountability Levels for non-Market Makers."
- **Member Agreement, market-maker tools** (read and quoted): the tools "may give market makers a trading advantage over members who are not market makers."
- **Counterpoint, so the picture is fair: Rulebook Rule 2.12(b), p. 22** (read and quoted): the affiliate must "Not have access to material non-public information, including but not limited to information regarding order flow, trading, settlement, contracts, and compliance".
- Not established: how much the affiliate trades. The article does not claim an amount.

## 3. The dispute process, its cost, and the lawyers

- **Rule 10.2(a)-(b), p. 69** (read and quoted): claims must be filed "within two years from the time the Claim arose." The non-refundable fee is "$500 for a Claim requesting relief totaling less than $5,000 in the aggregate"; for larger claims the fee is $1,000 (read in an earlier run's summary of the same rule).
- **Rule 10.2(c), p. 70** (read and quoted): "Kalshi shall promptly convene an arbitration panel." The fee is paid to Kalshi.
- **Rule 10.1(c), p. 69** (read and quoted): the winner can recover "all costs, expenses and reasonable attorneys' fees." This is the basis for "if you lose, you may also pay Kalshi's lawyers."
- **Rule 11.3(a), (c)-(d), pp. 73-74** (read and quoted): Kalshi is not liable for losses unless a final arbitration ruling finds it acted in a way that is "GROSSLY NEGLIGENT, RECKLESS, OR FRAUDULENT"; disputes go to "BINDING ARBITRATION" under New York law.
- **Arbitrator qualifications, Rule 10.5, p. 71:** an earlier run's summary says each arbitrator needs five years in financial services. Not quote-checked; confirm in the rule.
- **Open point:** whether you can choose arbitration. Rule 10.1(a) says "If so elected by a Participant," while Member Agreement §VII.A says you accept "the obligation to submit to arbitration." The article says disputes "go to arbitration" and does not call it optional.
- Who the panelists are and how they are picked beyond the qualification above: not established.

## 4. 43% of appealed pre-approval denials on HealthCare.gov plans were overturned (2025)

- **KFF, prior authorization brief, Aug 13, 2026, Key Takeaways** (read and quoted): "Sixty-seven percent of prior authorization denials were overturned upon appeal in Medicare Advantage, 47% were overturned upon appeal in Medicaid managed care, and 43% were overturned in the ACA federally facilitated Marketplace."
- Document: https://www.kff.org/patient-consumer-protections/prior-authorization-metrics-provide-new-insights-into-insurer-practices-but-gaps-remain/
- The figures come from insurers' own reports, and KFF's analysis covers 2025 data (KFF topic page and news coverage of the analysis).
- **Limit:** "overturned" does not mean the care was needed, and this is a different measure from the 66% "upheld" figure in KFF's claims-denial brief. KFF warns against averaging them.

## 5. Few patients appeal

- **KFF, "Claims Denials and Appeals in ACA Marketplace Plans in 2024," Appeals Data** (read and quoted): "Consumers rarely appeal denied claims (fewer than 1% of denied claims were appealed), and when they do, insurers usually uphold their original decision (66% of appeals were upheld)." Also: "consumers appealed at least 262,982 – an appeal rate of less than 1%."
- Document: https://www.kff.org/patient-consumer-protections/claims-denials-and-appeals-in-aca-marketplace-plans-in-2024/
- **Limit:** the verified figure is for denied claims, mostly after care. For pre-approval denials specifically, news coverage of KFF's analysis says appeals remain rare (secondary; snippet only). The KFF prior-authorization brief itself was not quoted on this point.

## 6. Outside review for $25 at most, and the insurer must accept the decision

- **HealthCare.gov, "External Review"** (read and quoted): "$25 per external review"; "Your insurer is required by law to accept the external reviewer’s decision."; "Insurance companies in all states must offer an external review process that meets the federal consumer protection standards."
- Cost detail (read and quoted): under the federal process there is "no charge"; "If your issuer has contracted with an independent review organization, or is using a state external review process, you may be charged." The $25 is the cap where a charge is allowed.
- Document: https://www.healthcare.gov/appeal-insurance-company-decision/external-review/
- **Order of steps** (read, summarized by the agent; HealthCare.gov appeals pages): an internal appeal to the insurer comes first, then outside review. https://www.healthcare.gov/appeal-insurance-company-decision/
- **"On medical grounds":** the agent's summary of the page is that outside review covers medical-judgment denials, and that a non-covered service often is not eligible. This is my reading; confirm the scope on the page and in your plan documents.
- **Reviewers are paid by the insurer in at least one state:** Pennsylvania Insurance Department (read and quoted): "The insurance company pays for completed reviews." The same page says the state "certifies that there are no conflicts of interest" between the review organization and the insurer. https://www.pa.gov/services/insurance/request-a-review-if-your-health-insurance-denied-a-treatment-medication-or-service

## 7. Insider trading is banned; there are reported cases; no independent evaluation of how well Kalshi stops it

**The ban**
- **Rulebook Rule 5.15(y), p. 54** (read and quoted): "An 'Insider' means any person who has access to or is in a position to have access to material nonpublic information before such information is made publicly available." The rule bars insiders from trading in that market.

**Reported cases** (secondary; check the CFTC's own releases)
- Blank Rome alert, Oct 14, 2026 (read in full this session): the CFTC treats insider trading in event contracts as fraud under Section 6(c)(1) of the Commodity Exchange Act and Rule 180.1, applies a "misappropriation" theory, and has brought four actions to date, some with parallel Justice Department charges. https://natlawreview.com/article/insider-trading-prediction-markets-cftc-and-doj-put-event-contracts-squarely
- Foley, May 2026 (snippet only): the CFTC announced its first insider-trading action involving event contracts on April 23, 2026. https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/05/insider-predicting-cftcs-first-action-and-how-we-got-here/
- CFTC Enforcement Division advisory on prediction markets (snippet only): describes a Kalshi penalty of $2,246.36 and a five-year suspension of a trader. https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9185-26
- Lowenstein client alert, Apr 2026 (snippet only): Kalshi and the CFTC announced disciplinary and enforcement actions on April 22 and 23, 2026.

**No independent evaluation of effectiveness**
- This is a negative finding. Searches of the CFTC, Kalshi, the Congressional Research Service and news coverage found no independent evaluation of how many insider trades go undetected or how well Kalshi's monitoring works.
- Kalshi describes its own measures on its site (identity checks, screening and blocking, surveillance, and employer details on higher-risk markets): https://kalshi.com/market-integrity/kyc-surveillance, https://kalshi.com/market-integrity/blocked-traders (snippet only). Kalshi-reported investigation counts reached the project through news coverage (tagged unconfirmed).
- A House Oversight Committee letter of May 2026 asked Kalshi how it verifies identity and detects anomalous trading, which suggests the answer is not public: https://oversight.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Kalshi-Prediction-Markets-Letter.pdf (snippet only).
- The Congressional Research Service has written that the open question is "whether and how" insider trading law applies to these markets (snippet only; the page blocked fetching): https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11406

## 8. Data: a perpetual license, and a rulebook that requires clear consent

- **Member Agreement §XI, p. 7** (read and quoted): you give Kalshi "a worldwide, perpetual, irrevocable, royalty-free, full sublicensable and freely assignable license" over your trade and order data, and "You acknowledge and agree that Kalshi may use such information for business, marketing and other purposes."

Also: "Kalshi shall not condition access to the Company upon a Participant's consent to the use of Proprietary Data and Personal Information for business or marketing purposes."
- **Which document wins** (read and quoted, Member Agreement §V.B, p. 2): "In the event of any conflict between this Agreement and the Kalshi Rulebook, the Kalshi Rulebook will govern."
- **Open point:** whether signing up counts as "clear consent." The run did not find "Proprietary Data" defined in the rulebook's definitions chapter. The article says this is not clear.
- **Contact:** the earlier run suggested writing to legal@kalshi.com, the privacy policy's contact. That address came from a page read in a run and was not re-confirmed (the live page returned an error when rechecked). Confirm before use. Privacy policy: https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-privacy-policy.html

## 9. Self-funded plans

- **What it means:** the employer pays claims itself, usually with an outside company running the plan. (KFF 2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey; Department of Labor booklet.)
- **Federal rules and the outside-review route depend on it.** Department of Labor, "Filing a Claim for Your Health Benefits," p. 9 (read and quoted): "The external review process used by the plan depends on whether the plan is self-funded or provides benefits through an insurance company." https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/publications/filing-a-claim-for-your-health-benefits.pdf
- **State rules may not reach them.** KFF 2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey (read and quoted): ERISA "exempts self-funded plans established by private employers (but not public employers) from most state insurance laws." https://www.kff.org/health-costs/2026-employer-health-benefits-survey/ Pennsylvania's state outside-review process excludes self-funded plans (Pennsylvania Insurance Department page above; my reading of the page).
- **Federal outside review may still apply.** The agent's brief corrected an earlier overstatement: the Department of Labor booklet shows self-funded plans can still owe an outside review under federal rules. The article says coverage "can differ, including access to outside review," not that it is unavailable.

## 11. Self-funded plans: the statistics and data gaps

- **No public denial data for them.** KFF prior-authorization brief (read and quoted): "There is no publicly available prior authorization data for self-funded plans sponsored by private employers."
- **State reporting laws exclude them.** KFF 2024 claims-denial brief (read and quoted): state laws "do not apply to self-funded health plans sponsored by private employers."
- **The federal requirement is not in place.** KFF 2024 claims-denial brief, Looking Forward (read and quoted): "the ACA requires group health plans to disclose claims denial and other data; however, these requirements still have not been implemented at the federal level." The source is from 2024; check whether this has changed.
- **Share of workers:** an earlier run's summary says 71% of covered workers are in self-funded plans. Not quote-checked; the article does not use this number. Check KFF's 2026 survey before citing it.
- **Fact-check example in the article:** the prototype wrote that employer plans publish no denial data, and its fact checker found the source said this applies only to self-funded plans (agent's run on health insurance denials and costs).

## 12. H.R. 9396 (used in the article's "To change" step)

- **Bill text** (read in full this session): "Prior Authorization Accountability Act," introduced June 23, 2026 by Rep. Goldman (R-TX), referred to the Energy and Commerce, Ways and Means, and Education and Workforce committees. https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr9396/BILLS-119hr9396ih.htm
- It would require a "group health plan or health insurance issuer" that imposes prior authorization to publish: approval and denial rates (overall and by item and service), the share of denials that were appealed, the share of resolved appeals that resulted in approval, response times, and the share of requests decided solely through decision-support, artificial-intelligence or machine-learning technology. It applies to plan years beginning on or after January 1, 2027.
- It covers prior authorization, not all claim denials. Whether the term "group health plan" reaches self-funded plans is my reading of the text; confirm.
- The bill's current stage is not confirmed. The article makes no claim about it.

The transcripts are not published; the counts can be reproduced by running the agent (`si_agent.py`) on the same questions.
