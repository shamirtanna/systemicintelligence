## 1. Key points

- **Your denial reason decides your path.** An outside review covers medical-judgment denials, and a non-covered service often isn't eligible.
  - So what: read the reason on the letter first.
- **Ask for the insurer's rules and your claim file in writing, today.** It's free.
  - So what: you can match your doctor's letter to the insurer's own criteria.
- **Two clocks matter.** You have at least 180 days to file an internal appeal, and 4 months from the final denial to ask for outside review.
  - So what: write both dates down the day the letter arrives.
- **If delay could hurt you, ask your doctor to certify urgency.** Decisions then come in as little as 72 hours.
  - So what: urgency is what moves an appeal from weeks to days.
- **Nobody publishes MRI denial rates.** Federal reports don't break out MRIs, and self-funded employer plans publish no data.
  - So what: you can't know your odds, so ask your insurer, your state, and CMS (the federal agency that runs Medicare and Medicaid) to publish them.

## 9. Glossary

- **Appeal (internal):** asking your insurer to re-review its own decision.
- **CMS:** the federal agency that runs Medicare and Medicaid.
- **DOL / EBSA:** the Department of Labor and its Employee Benefits Security Administration, which oversees private employer plans.
- **EOB (Explanation of Benefits):** the statement the insurer sends after processing a claim.
- **ERISA:** the 1974 federal law that sets claim and appeal rules for employer plans.
- **External review:** an independent party reviews a denial, and the insurer must follow the result.
- **Grandfathered plan:** a plan that predates the 2010 health law and hasn't changed much.
- **Independent review organization (IRO):** the outside group that decides an external review.
- **Post-service claim:** a claim for care already received. **Pre-service claim:** a request for approval before care.
- **Prior authorization:** the insurer's approval, needed before some care.
- **Self-funded plan:** the employer pays claims itself, and an outside company usually administers the plan.
